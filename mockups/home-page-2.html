Home page: round two

As you asked, the desks come first. There are no counts, and one short line on what the site is and how it works, with a link to the full methodology. The side menu and its button stay as they are now, and there is no date. Each option is shown at PC width and at phone width. The map is real; its pins here are examples.

What similar sites do.

ISWA one-sentence mission at the top, then its "teams" as large map shapes of each region (Russia and Ukraine, the Middle East, China and Taiwan). The regions are the way in.
ACLEDOne line on what ACLED is, beside a world map of recent events with a small filter. Below that, a card per crisis ("Iran war updates").
LiveuamapThe map is the whole page, with a live news column on the right. Fast, but nothing tells a new reader what the site is.
Crisis Group's CrisisWatchOne entry per conflict: a bold summary line, then short notes. Plain, and quick to scan.
BellingcatA magazine front page of investigations. It fits stories more than live desks, so I took little from it.
Critical ThreatsBehind a bot check; not viewed.

From these I took the desk as the way in (ISW), one map of the region (ACLED, Liveuamap), and a short line per desk (CrisisWatch). My pick: H2. The map shows at once where each desk works, and the desks sit beside it. H1 is the calmest, and H3 the quickest to read.
