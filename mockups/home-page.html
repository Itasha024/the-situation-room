The home page: three options

What a reader sees on thesituationroom.live. Options 1 and 2 are a real front page that leads to the desks. Option 3 opens a desk straight away, with a thin strip of desks above it. All three use the same masthead and the faded flags from the header mockup. I'd go with 1: it reads like a newspaper's front page, shows that the site is live, and adding a third desk is just one more column.

1 · Front page

One column per desk, with its flag faded in the header, its status and its local clock. Under that: the first point of its Latest developments with its source, and the day's counts. Below the columns, "Across the desks" lists the newest reports from every desk, each marked with its desk's colour. A desk being built carries an amber dot. The next desk shows as a quiet placeholder until it opens.

2 · Situation board

A dark map of the region leads, with each desk's area lit in its flag colours. Clicking an area opens that desk. Beside the map, one row per desk gives its status, clock and first point. It is the most "situation room" of the three, but on a phone the map takes the first screen.

3 · A desk as the home page

No front page: the site opens the desk the reader used last, or the busiest desk for a first visit. A thin "Desks" strip above the header switches between them in one tap. It is the quickest way into the news, but there is no overview of the desks side by side.
