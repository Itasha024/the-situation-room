The home page: more options

What a reader sees on thesituationroom.live. As you asked: no date under the masthead, the menu button on the left as now, option 3 gone. 1 and 2 stay; 4, 5 and 6 are new, and the features they carry can be mixed into any of them (listed at the end). I'd go with 5: the desks side by side like 1, with the day's pulse of each desk, a combined live feed and a short "since your last visit" line.

1 · Front page

One column per desk, with its flag faded in the header, its status and its local clock. Under that: the first point of its Latest developments with its source, and the day's counts. Below the columns, "Across the desks" lists the newest reports from every desk, each marked with its desk's colour. A desk being built carries an amber dot. The next desk shows as a quiet placeholder until it opens.

2 · Situation board

A dark map of the region leads, with each desk's area lit in its flag colours. Clicking an area opens that desk. Beside the map, one row per desk gives its status, clock and first point. It is the most "situation room" of the three, but on a phone the map takes the first screen.

4 · The morning briefing

The page opens on one short briefing across every desk, refreshed every 3 hours: four or five numbered points, each tagged with its desk and its source, the way Latest developments works. Under it, one compact card per desk. It answers "what happened today in the region" in ten seconds, before the reader picks a desk.

5 · Desks with their pulse (my pick)

Like 1, a column per desk, with three things added. A 24-hour pulse under each desk's name: reports per hour, attacks marked in the desk's colour, so a busy night shows at a glance. A "since your last visit" line (12 new reports, 3 attacks), kept in the reader's own browser, no account. And one live feed below, from every desk, with a button per desk to filter it.

6 · The live wire

For the reader who wants the news now: the page is one live feed from every desk, newest first, the latest big event as a banner on top. A narrow column on the right holds each desk with its clock and counts, and leads into it. On a phone the desks become a row above the feed.

Features any option can carry

Pick the ones you want; each is free to run.

Search every deskOne box on the home page, the same smart search as the desks, across all of them.
Since your last visitNew reports and attacks per desk since the reader last came, kept in their browser only.
The 24-hour pulseReports per hour per desk, attacks marked: how busy each war was overnight.
One mapA small map of the region with each desk's latest pins; a tap opens the desk on that pin.
Key numbersA strip of each desk's headline figures (ships hit, strikes today, Hormuz traffic), each with its source.
Remember my deskA returning reader is offered "Back to the Iran desk" at the top, one tap.
Languages and themesEnglish, Hebrew and Arabic, and the same themes as the desks, from the menu.
About and methodA short line on what the site is and how it works, leading to each desk's methodology.
