A strategy-game look for the desks

Open the working mock →

Strategy games solved our problem years ago: show a big, busy map without losing the reader. The mock puts the map right under each desk's headline and flies the map to a desk when you open it. The home page starts with one map for all desks, then the latest reports from every desk together. Below are all the ideas, each with where it comes from.

My picks

The six I would build first. They fit the site's rules (no counts that need explaining, no commentary) and most are small.

01In the mockMedium

Map under the headline, fly to the desk

Supreme Commander's strategic zoom · Google Earth

One map stays on the page. Clicking a desk (tab, map plate or key 1/2) flies the map from the whole region to that desk, and the headline and feed change with it. No page reload, so it feels like one place.

02In the mockSmall

Latest alert button (Space)

StarCraft · Warcraft III

One press flies the map to the newest report and opens it; pressing again steps back through older ones. New reports also slide in as small chips at the map's edge ("+2 new · Hormuz") that jump to the place when clicked.

03In the mockMedium

Map modes

Hearts of Iron IV · Europa Universalis · Civilization VI lenses

Same map, different question. Reports (pins), Activity heat (where it is busiest this week), Control (who holds what), Quiet areas (no confirmed reports for 7 days, dimmed, so empty means "no news", not "calm"). Keys Q W E R.

04In the mockMedium

Replay strip

Game replays · Flightradar24 playback · Windy · ISW time-lapse

A strip under the map with one bar per day since the war began. Drag it to see any week, or press play to watch the war unfold day by day. Replaces today's Back / date / Forward buttons with one control that also shows where the busy days were.

05In the mockSmall

Since your last visit

Game "while you were away" screens

"Since your last visit (6 h): 14 new · 1 control change." New pins glow and new cards get a small NEW mark. Kept in the reader's own browser, no accounts.

06In the mockSmall

Home: desk plates on the map

CFR Global Conflict Tracker · CrisisWatch arrows

Each desk sits on the home map as a small plate: flag, its top line now, a 30-day activity line and an arrow (busier or calmer than last week). Click the plate and the map flies in. On a phone the plates become two buttons over the map.

From strategy games

What each game does, and what it would be on our site.

07In the mockSmall

Minimap

StarCraft · Age of Empires

A small map of the whole theatre in the corner, with a box showing where you are. Click it to move. Useful once you zoom into a town and lose the big picture.

08In the mockSmall

Top bar of key figures

Age of Empires resource bar

A thin bar over the map: reports in 24 h, strikes in 24 h, day of the war, and one desk figure (oil price for Iran). Few, plain, with the change since yesterday.

09In the mockMedium

Selection card

StarCraft command card

Clicking a pin opens one fixed card at the map's corner, never a pop-up over the map: the report, its time and place, a strip showing who reported it (official, wire, local, a party), and buttons: nearby in 7 days, same actor, read, share this view.

10In the mockMedium

Command layout

Command & Conquer sidebar

A second layout: the map fills the screen and the feed sits in a fixed right column with tabs (Reports, Log, Numbers, Fronts). For readers who keep the site open all day. Toggle at the top of the mock.

11Medium

Turning-point screen

Hearts of Iron IV news events

A rare full-width moment for real turning points (a ceasefire, a capital struck, a new front): one photo, one headline, the date. At most once a week, so it keeps its weight.

12Medium

Overnight moves

Total War end-of-turn camera

On the first visit of the day, a 10-second replay of the last 24 hours plays on the map (pins appear in order, control changes fade in). One tap skips it.

13Medium

Launch-to-impact arcs

DEFCON

When a report names both the launch area and the target (a Houthi missile from Saada at Jizan), draw a thin arc between them. Only when both are stated; otherwise just the impact pin.

14Small

Log view

Command: Modern Operations message log

A dense one-line-per-report view (time, type, headline) as an option beside the cards, for power users. In the mock's Command layout under the cards.

15Big

The Ledger

Europa Universalis ledger

A tables page per desk: provinces by number of strikes, actors by attacks, week by week, sortable and downloadable. Turns the map into something researchers can cite.

16Medium

Actors roster

Unit cards in RTS games

One card per force (Houthi forces, government forces, IRGC, the coalition...): emblem, where it was active this week on a small map, its latest claims and its latest reported losses.

17Small

Keyboard play

Every RTS

0 home, 1 Yemen, 2 Iran, Q W E R map modes, Space for the latest alert, ← → for days. Shown as small key marks only on a computer.

New ideas of our own

Things none of these sites do, that fit what the desk already has.

18Medium

Links between desks

When a report belongs to two desks (an Iranian diplomat on Saudi–Yemen mediation), the home map draws a thin line between the two desks and the card carries both desk marks. Shows the region as one system.

19Medium

Knock-on chain

Follow one event's effects: Hormuz attack → oil above $98 → flights to Riyadh suspended. Cards linked in a short chain under the first report, each with its own source.

20Medium

Place pages

Click a town's name anywhere: its own page with every pin and report there since the war began, its control history and a small map. Good for search engines too ("Marib latest").

21Medium

Claimed vs confirmed pins

A claim (one side only) is a hollow pin; once an official body or a wire confirms it, it fills in. The reader sees at a glance what is still only a claim, without any label in the copy.

22Small

Story threads

A long story (Hormuz shipping, Marib offensive) gets a thread: all its cards in order on one strip, with the map showing only its pins. Opened from any card of the thread.

23Small

Share this exact view

The link keeps the desk, the day, the map mode and the zoom. "The Control map of Taiz on 3 October" becomes one link anyone can open.

24Medium

Two dates side by side

A swipe line across the map: left is one date, right is another. Made for control changes ("Marib 1 Sept vs today").

25Small

Watch a place

Star a town, port or ship. A small badge on its desk tab when something new lands there. Browser-only, no account.

26Medium

Hour-of-day clock

A round 24-hour chart per desk showing when attacks happen (night raids, dawn launches). Answers "when" the way the map answers "where".

27Medium

Embeds for other sites

A "last 24 hours" map and a figures strip that other sites and journalists can place on their pages, credited to the desk.

28Small

Daily map card

At 00:00 the desk makes a square image: today's map, three lines, the date. Ready for X and Telegram, like the war-map channels post every morning.

29Big

Globe opening

The home page opens on a slowly turning globe that settles on the region, then the desks light up one by one. Only on the first visit; skipped after.

30Small

Situation-room sound

Off by default. When on, a soft tick for each new report while the tab is open, a lower tone for a strike. For people who keep the desk in a side tab.
