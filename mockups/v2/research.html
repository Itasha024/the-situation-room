One newsroom, many fronts

What the best conflict sites and strategy games do, old and new, and why the mock now has one main desk with countries and seas you focus on, and wars as their own pages.

Open mock v2 →   v1 (separate desks)

The short answer

Yes, make one main desk. But keep the wars as pages of their own. Every mature product ends up with two separate axes:

  • Where it happened: a country or a sea. This is the stable way readers move around: "Yemen", "Lebanon", "Red Sea".
  • Which war it belongs to: a curated story with its own fronts, control map, numbers, timeline and rules: "Yemen war", "Iran war".

One report can sit on both axes, and on several countries at once. An Iranian diplomat on Saudi–Yemen mediation is tagged Iran, Saudi Arabia and Yemen, and belongs to the Yemen war. It is one card with one link, shown wherever it belongs, never copied.

Three ways to build it

A

Separate desks + a shared home

Today's site plus a home page (mock v1).

Least work and each war keeps its identity. But there is no Lebanon, Iraq or Gulf focus without building new desks, cross-border reports get copied, and every new war becomes another separate silo.
B

One desk, countries only

The Liveuamap model: one map, and regions are saved views of it.

Simplest to understand, and exactly the spitball. But the Yemen war loses its home: its fronts, control map, totals and timeline. Search also has no "Yemen war" page to find.
C · recommended

One desk; countries as focus, wars as stories

Built in mock v2.

Gives "a desk per country inside a main desk" and keeps today's two desks as the war pages. It also gives search the most pages to find, and a report about several countries shows up in each of them as the same single card.

Who does what: Al Jazeera separates /where/yemen (place) from /tag/israel-iran-conflict (story). AP has country hubs and story hubs. Wikipedia has country articles, war articles and an umbrella "Middle East crisis" page. ACLED keeps one global dataset with a "Yemen Conflict Monitor" built on top of it. ISW's Iran Update is one daily product with a section for each country.

What the games teach

The closest model is esports spectator design, not the games themselves. Broadcasters show a complex match to people who are not playing, which is exactly a news reader's position. They remove the player's controls and keep one line of state that can be read from across the room.

What we must not take from games

  • Scores, kill feeds, death totals as the headline number, leaderboards, "war score".
  • Red for enemy and blue for friend. That takes a side. Colours here mean the type of event, and side colours appear only in the Sides mode.
  • Sound, screen shake, flashes, glowing explosions. So I dropped the "situation-room sound" idea from v1. The pulsing ring on new pins is now a soft one.
  • Military cosplay ("TOP SECRET", stencil fonts), achievements, "missions".

Found on the way

  • One event, 25 cards. The Al-Maasheeq Palace attack produced 25 Yemen cards in seven hours (reactions, casualty updates, re-tellings). The "Developing story" block makes this readable. The bigger question is whether the feed should merge more of these at write time.
  • Most Iran reports have no place. Talks, sanctions and statements make up most of the Iran desk, so its map is thin in a 24-hour window. Country pages handle this well: the wire and the brief carry it, and the map is not the only view.
  • Control lives only in Yemen. For the Iran war, the honest map layer is "Sides" (who is on which side), not control. The mock labels it that way.

If we build it, in this order

  1. Give every report countries, wars and a category; most of this comes from the desk and the pin it already has.
  2. Build the main page and the country and sea pages on one shared map and feed.
  3. Turn today's two desks into the war pages, with the old addresses forwarding to them.
  4. Then the extras: place pages, dated briefs, replay, compare, following, tools.
